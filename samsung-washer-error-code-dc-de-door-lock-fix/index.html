Samsung Washer dC / dE / dE1 Error: Door Lock Solenoid & Harness Test

By Fault and Fix Diagnostic Engineering Team
Updated: Nov 8, 2026
Reviewed by Marcus Vance (Senior Appliance Specialist)
5 min read
Verified OEM Fault Diagnostic dC / dE / dE1
Verified against Samsung OEM Engineering Service Specs
Official Fault Meaning: Door Interlock / Lid Switch Open Failure (PCB fails to confirm closed door contacts prior to cycle start)
Most Likely Root Cause: Burnt door lock solenoid coil, broken door latch strike hook, or tarnished microswitch contacts.
Repair Difficulty: Intermediate DIY
Estimated Time: 20–30 minutes
Required Tools: Phillips #2 screwdriver, digital multimeter, small flat-head screwdriver
Applicable Models: Samsung WF42H5000, WF45R6100, WA50R5400, WF45K6500 series
Immediate Safe Action / 60-Second Quick Check

Check for caught clothing in door bellow, push firmly on door handle until a solid mechanical click sounds, and restart.

1. Symptoms & Operational Failure Signs

When code dC / dE / dE1 triggers, the central microcontroller detects anomalous feedback or sensor voltage out of range. Check for these symptoms:

  • Washer chimes twice and will not engage water fill valves when Start is pressed
  • Lock icon on display flashes continuously or fails to illuminate
  • Display flashes 'dC' (Door Check) on top-loaders or 'dE / dE1' on front-loaders
  • Door opens freely even during high-speed wash drum rotation

2. The 30-Second Hard Reset Sequence

Clear temporary software lockouts, residual filter capacitor voltage, and transient logic anomalies with this factory capacitive drain routine:

  1. Press Power button to turn off unit. Unplug power cord from wall outlet.
  2. Open door/lid and inspect the door strike bracket for cracks or misalignment.
  3. Firmly open and re-close the door 5 consecutive times to mechanically exercise latch microswitch.
  4. Reconnect power, hold Start button for 3 seconds, and verify solenoid click engages.

3. Entering Hidden Factory Diagnostic Mode

Execute this technician keypad/dial cadence to force direct component test routines, cycle individual actuators, and read stored historical fault codes:

  1. Enter technician diagnostic mode: Press 'Spin' + 'Soil Level' + 'Power' simultaneously.
  2. Check code history: Turn rotary cycle dial to view active latch feedback flag 'd0'.
  3. Actuator test: Press 'Temp' key to toggle door lock triac output on and off.
  4. Listen for sharp solenoid click; verify digital readout toggles between 'LO' (Locked) and 'UL' (Unlocked).

4. Multimeter Resistance (Ω) Specifications & Harness Pinouts

Disconnect electrical power before probing harness terminals. Set your digital multimeter to the lowest Ohms (Ω) scale and test the critical circuit path:

5. Step-by-Step Mechanical Tear-Down & Repair

Follow these 5 OEM service manual steps to access, inspect, and replace the failing component safely:

  1. Spring Retainer Removal: Unplug washer. Locate door boot gasket outer spring clamp at 6 o'clock position. Pry spring carefully with flat screwdriver and peel clamp away.
  2. Gasket Peel Back: Peel rubber bellows gasket off front cabinet lip around right latch receiver opening.
  3. Latch Mounting Screws: Remove two Phillips #2 screws securing door latch mechanism to front cabinet frame.
  4. Harness Extraction: Reach behind front panel, pull latch mechanism into opening, release locking tab, and disconnect 3-pin wiring harness.
  5. Installation & Strike Check: Connect replacement OEM door latch (DC34-00025A / DC64-00519D), screw into frame, reseat gasket lip, and verify strike alignment.

6. Frequently Asked Questions

Can I bypass the door lock switch with a jumper wire?

Never jump Samsung door lock switches. The main PCB looks for synchronized timing between the solenoid firing pulse and the secondary feedback contact closure; a permanent jumper triggers fault code dC immediately.

Why does error dC appear even after replacing the lock assembly?

If a new lock does not resolve dC, check the door hinge. A sagging door misaligns the strike by 1/8 inch, preventing the mechanical slide bar from reaching full extension inside the latch switch cavity.
