LG Dishwasher bE Error Code: Excessive Suds & Foaming Diagnostic

By Fault and Fix Diagnostic Engineering Team
Updated: Nov 16, 2026
Reviewed by Marcus Vance (Senior Appliance Specialist)
5 min read
Verified OEM Fault Diagnostic bE
Verified against LG OEM Engineering Service Specs
Official Fault Meaning: Excessive Detergent Foaming / Bubble Error (Suds Detected by Motor Current or Turbidity Sensor)
Most Likely Root Cause: Accidental use of liquid hand dish soap (Dawn), overdosing high-surfactant gel pods, rinse aid reservoir leak, or grease-coated turbidity sensor.
Repair Difficulty: Intermediate DIY
Estimated Time: 20 mins
Required Tools: 1 Cup Vegetable Oil or Milk, Sponge, Shallow Pan, Digital Multimeter
Applicable Models: LDT7808ST, LDFN4542S, LDP6797ST, LDT5678ST, LDF5545ST, LDTS5552S
Immediate Safe Action / 60-Second Quick Check

Open dishwasher door carefully to avoid suds spilling onto the floor. Do not run normal cycles with foam present.

1. Symptoms & Operational Failure Signs

When code bE triggers, the central microcontroller detects anomalous feedback or sensor voltage out of range. Check for these symptoms:

  • 'bE' flashing on control panel; wash motor surges and cavitates with high-pitched whining
  • Dense white soap foam pouring out from bottom door corners onto floor
  • Wash cycle stalls repeatedly during pre-wash or main wash agitation phase
  • Drain pump unable to expel foam because air bubbles cause impeller cavitation

2. The 30-Second Hard Reset Sequence

Clear temporary software lockouts, residual filter capacitor voltage, and transient logic anomalies with this factory capacitive drain routine:

  1. Open the dishwasher door and scoop out as much foam as possible using a bowl and sponge.
  2. Pour 1/2 cup of standard vegetable cooking oil or 2 cups of whole milk directly into the tub bottom (oil instantly collapses foam bubbles).
  3. Close the door, select 'Quick' or 'Rinse Only' cycle, and allow the machine to run for 3 minutes to circulate oil and knock down suds.
  4. Cancel the cycle to force a drain. Repeat once with plain cold water to flush residue.

3. Entering Hidden Factory Diagnostic Mode

Execute this technician keypad/dial cadence to force direct component test routines, cycle individual actuators, and read stored historical fault codes:

  1. Inspect the detergent dispenser door latch and rinse aid cap. Ensure the rinse aid gasket is not cracked or leaking fluid.
  2. Inspect the optical turbidity sensor located in the sump bowl below the lower spray arm filter.
  3. Verify wash motor amperage: excessive foaming reduces motor drag, causing motor RPM to spike, which the controller flags as bE.
  4. Check the optical sensor harness for detergent residue.

5. Step-by-Step Mechanical Tear-Down & Repair

Follow these 5 OEM service manual steps to access, inspect, and replace the failing component safely:

  1. Suds Neutralization: Scoop foam, apply 1/2 cup vegetable oil across sump, and run a drain sequence.
  2. Filter Removal: Unlock and lift out the cylindrical stainless-steel micro-filter and flat screen.
  3. Turbidity Sensor Clean: Wipe the clear plastic optical eye inside the sump bowl with a soft microfiber cloth and isopropyl alcohol.
  4. Rinse Aid Inspection: If rinse aid compartment leaks continuously, replace dispenser assembly door seal.
  5. Verification Cycle: Run a full empty cycle on 'Normal' with no soap to ensure clear water circulation and zero error recurrence.

6. Frequently Asked Questions

Why does vegetable oil eliminate dishwasher suds so fast?

Vegetable oil acts as a powerful non-toxic surfactant breaker. The lipids drastically alter water surface tension, immediately popping microscopic soap bubbles and allowing the drain pump to grip solid liquid rather than cavitating in air foam.

Can too much rinse aid cause the bE error?

Yes. If the rinse aid dispenser dial is set too high (level 4 or 5) or if the reservoir rubber seal is leaking, excess surfactant is released into every cycle, creating heavy foaming and triggering the bE code.
