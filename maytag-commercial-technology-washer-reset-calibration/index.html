Maytag Washer Stuck on “Sensing”: Shift Actuator Diagnosis & Calibration Reset

By admin
Updated: Oct 13, 2026
Reviewed by Dan Miller (Master Appliance Tech)
3 min read
Verified Fault Diagnostic STUCK-SENSING
Verified by Maytag / Whirlpool Tech Service Sheet W10913953
Official Fault Meaning: Optical Tachometer / Transmission Splutch Synchronization Failure (F7 E1 / F7 E5)
Most Likely Cause: Failed shift actuator motor, cracked splutch slider teeth, grease contamination on optical tachometer sensor, or control board calibration drift.
Repair Difficulty: Intermediate DIY
Estimated Time: 35–45 minutes
Required Tools: 5/16" and 7/16" nut drivers, small flathead screwdriver, work light, protective gloves, multimeter
Applicable Models: Maytag Commercial Technology (MCT), Maytag Centennial MVWC415, MVWX655, Bravos XL; Whirlpool WTW4800, WTW4900, WTW5000
Immediate Safe Action / Quick Reset

Run Automatic Recalibration Mode: From Diagnostic Mode (1L-3R-1L-1R), turn knob until ONLY the "Rinse" light illuminates. Press START. Let washer run empty calibration cycle for 3 minutes without touching.

A Maytag Commercial Technology (MCT), Centennial, or Bravos washer that fills with a burst of water, clicks underneath, and remains indefinitely stuck on the green “Sensing” status light is experiencing a drive-train synchronization failure. The washer’s central computer cannot determine whether the transmission is geared for agitation or spinning.

In 9 out of 10 cases, this symptom corresponds to diagnostic fault codes F7 E1 (Tachometer Speed Sensor Error) or F7 E5 (Shifter State Fault), caused by a failing Shift Actuator mounted underneath the tub.

What the Shift Actuator Does

Beneath the washing machine drum, a small red-and-white plastic box called the Shift Actuator houses two critical components:

  • Synchronous Motor: Physically shifts the plastic cam (the splutch) between wash agitation mode and high-speed spin mode.
  • Optical Tachometer Sensor: An infrared sensor that reads a slotted disc on the motor pulley to calculate drum rotation speed down to the millisecond.

When the internal motor burns out or detergent scum coats the optical eye, the control board halts the washer on “Sensing” to prevent transmission shredding.

Testing the Shift Actuator with a Multimeter

⚠️ Disconnect Power

Unplug the machine before tilting it backward. Do NOT lay the washer completely flat on its back; water from the balance ring may flood the control board.

  1. Tilt the washer backward 45 degrees and prop securely.
  2. Locate the shift actuator mounted adjacent to the drive belt and main pulley.
  3. Remove the two 5/16″ hex screws and unplug the 6-pin wire harness.
  4. Set your multimeter to the Ohms (Ω) setting.
  5. Measure across Pins 2 and 3 on the shift actuator (the synchronous motor terminals):
Test Terminals Component Normal OEM Value Verdict
Pins 2 to 3 Shift Actuator Motor 2,000 Ω to 3,000 Ω (2k–3k Ω) PASS: Motor coil is functional.
Pins 2 to 3 Shift Actuator Motor Open Loop (O.L.) / 0 Ω FAIL: Internal synchronous motor burned. Replace actuator.
Splutch Teeth Plastic Cam Slider Sharp, square teeth If teeth are rounded/stripped, replace splutch kit #W10721967.

Running the Automatic Calibration Mode

Whenever you replace the shift actuator or the washer experiences an off-balance shake, you MUST execute the calibration routine:

  1. Ensure the tub is 100% empty and the lid is closed.
  2. Turn the cycle knob: 1 Left, 3 Right, 1 Left, 1 Right within 6 seconds.
  3. Turn the dial clockwise until ONLY the Rinse LED is illuminated.
  4. Press START.
  5. Step away. The washer will automatically actuate the motor, calibrate the splutch positions, and unlock the lid after approximately 2.5 minutes.

OEM Replacement Part Information

OEM Genuine Part

Maytag / Whirlpool Washer Shift Actuator

OEM Part Number: W10913953 (Replaces WPW10006355, W10006355, AP6037249)

Compatibility: Maytag Commercial Technology (MVWC200–MVWX655), Centennial, Bravos; Whirlpool WTW4800, WTW5000 series.

Replacement Time: 20 minutes with a 5/16″ nut driver.
