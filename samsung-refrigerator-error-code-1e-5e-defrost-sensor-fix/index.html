Samsung Refrigerator 1E / 5E Error Code: Defrost Sensor & Bi-Metal Test

By Fault and Fix Diagnostic Engineering Team
Updated: Nov 9, 2026
Reviewed by Marcus Vance (Senior Appliance Specialist)
5 min read
Verified OEM Fault Diagnostic 1E / 5E
Verified against Samsung OEM Engineering Service Specs
Official Fault Meaning: Defrost Sensor Open / Short Circuit (1E indicates Freezer Defrost Sensor fault; 5E indicates Refrigerator Compartment Defrost Sensor fault)
Most Likely Root Cause: Moisture intrusion into epoxy defrost sensor capsule (DA32-00006W), cracked sensor casing, or blown thermal fuse.
Repair Difficulty: Intermediate DIY
Estimated Time: 30–45 minutes
Required Tools: Phillips screwdriver, digital multimeter, heat gun or hair dryer, ice bath bucket
Applicable Models: Samsung RF28R7351, RF263BEAESR, RF28HMEDBSR, RS25J500D series
Immediate Safe Action / 60-Second Quick Check

Inspect rear interior panel of affected compartment for thick snow/ice buildup choking off cold airflow vents.

1. Symptoms & Operational Failure Signs

When code 1E / 5E triggers, the central microcontroller detects anomalous feedback or sensor voltage out of range. Check for these symptoms:

  • Freezer or fresh food section gradually warms up to 50°F while compressor runs non-stop
  • Display flashes '1E' (Freezer Defrost) or '5E' (Fridge Defrost) following power outage
  • Thick layer of frost or solid rime ice visible through evaporator back cover vents
  • Evaporator fan blades scrape noisily against frost buildup before jamming

2. The 30-Second Hard Reset Sequence

Clear temporary software lockouts, residual filter capacitor voltage, and transient logic anomalies with this factory capacitive drain routine:

  1. Press and hold 'Power Freeze' and 'Power Cool' buttons simultaneously for 8 seconds.
  2. Display will chime and reset display panel back to normal temperature setpoint display.
  3. If error returns immediately, the sensor resistance is permanently outside valid parameters.
  4. Perform manual forced defrost by pressing buttons until 'Fd' appears on screen.

3. Entering Hidden Factory Diagnostic Mode

Execute this technician keypad/dial cadence to force direct component test routines, cycle individual actuators, and read stored historical fault codes:

  1. Enter technician diagnostic: Hold 'Energy Saver' + 'Lighting' (or 'Power Freeze' + 'Lighting') for 8 seconds.
  2. All LED segments will flash; any steady lit segment identifies exact failing sensor.
  3. Segment 1 corresponds to 1E (Freezer Defrost Sensor).
  4. Segment 5 corresponds to 5E (Refrigerator Defrost Sensor).

4. Multimeter Resistance (Ω) Specifications & Harness Pinouts

Disconnect electrical power before probing harness terminals. Set your digital multimeter to the lowest Ohms (Ω) scale and test the critical circuit path:

5. Step-by-Step Mechanical Tear-Down & Repair

Follow these 5 OEM service manual steps to access, inspect, and replace the failing component safely:

  1. Safety & Food Removal: Unplug refrigerator power cord. Transfer perishable food to coolers. Remove freezer bins and shelves.
  2. Evaporator Cover Removal: Remove 2 to 4 Phillips screws securing rear plastic evaporator cover. Carefully unplug fan motor harness connector.
  3. Ice De-Icing: Do not pull frozen panel with force! Use a steamer or hair dryer on low to thaw frozen drain trough and release cover.
  4. Sensor Extraction: Locate defrost sensor clipped to top right tube bend of aluminum evaporator coil. Snip zip tie holding wiring harness.
  5. Installation & Seal: Clip new OEM defrost sensor (DA32-00006W) securely to suction line, connect wiring plug, replace thermal fuse if blown, and reassemble.

6. Frequently Asked Questions

Can I splice in a generic defrost sensor?

Never use generic sensors. Samsung microprocessors use an exact proprietary NTC resistance curve (5.0 kΩ at 77°F, 13.3 kΩ at 32°F). Generic 10 kΩ sensors will misreport temperatures by 30°F, preventing defrost termination.

Why does ice build up behind the panel even after replacing the sensor?

Check the evaporator defrost drain hole at the bottom of the coil. If the metal drain trough heater clip (DA61-06796A) is missing or displaced, melted defrost water refreezes in the drain hole, damming up future cycles.
